The PLONK verification algorithm is a cornerstone of modern zk-SNARKs, enabling succinct, non-interactive proofs of arbitrary computations. Here we break it down step by step.

At a high level, PLONK verification ensures that the proof generated by the prover is valid for a specific computation. The verifier performs various checks, using cryptographic commitments and field operations to validate the proof without re-executing the computation.

Before diving into the steps, let's clarify the inputs:

- **Verifier preprocessed input:** Commitments to the selector polynomials $[q_M]_1, [q_L]_1, [q_R]_1, [q_O]_1, [q_C]_1$, commitments to the permutation polynomials $[s_{\sigma_1}]_1, [s_{\sigma_2}]_1, [s_{\sigma_3}]_1$, and $[x]_2$, the secret point of the setup in $\mathbb{G}_2$.
- **Proof elements** ($\pi_{\text{SNARK}}$): The commitments $[a]_1, [b]_1, [c]_1, [z]_1, [t_{lo}]_1, [t_{mid}]_1, [t_{hi}]_1, [W_\zeta]_1, [W_{\zeta\omega}]_1$, and the field elements $\bar{a}, \bar{b}, \bar{c}, \bar{s}_{\sigma_1}, \bar{s}_{\sigma_2}, \bar{z}_\omega$.
- **Challenges** ($\beta, \gamma, \alpha, \zeta, v, u$): Generated deterministically from the circuit, the public inputs and the proof.

### Step-by-Step Breakdown

**1. Validate the proof components.** The verifier starts by checking that the proof elements are well formed:

- All nine commitments are valid points of $\mathbb{G}_1$.
- All six evaluations are elements of $\mathbb{F}$.
- The public inputs $(w_i)_{i \in [\ell]}$ are elements of $\mathbb{F}$.

**2. Compute the challenges.** The challenges $\beta, \gamma, \alpha, \zeta, v, u$ are derived by replaying the transcript exactly as the prover did.

**3. Evaluate polynomials.** Three evaluations are computed:

- **Zero polynomial**, which vanishes on the roots of unity: $Z_H(\zeta) = \zeta^n - 1$
- **First Lagrange polynomial:** $L_1(\zeta) = \dfrac{\omega (\zeta^n - 1)}{n (\zeta - \omega)}$
- **Public input polynomial**, using the sign convention from the prover: $\text{PI}(\zeta) = -\sum_{i \in [\ell]} w_i L_i(\zeta)$

**4. Split $r$, and compute the batched commitment $[D]_1$.** The verifier splits the polynomial $r$ into a constant part and a part that depends on commitments. The constant part is:

$$r_0 = \text{PI}(\zeta) - L_1(\zeta)\alpha^2 - \alpha (\bar{a} + \beta \bar{s}_{\sigma_1} + \gamma)(\bar{b} + \beta \bar{s}_{\sigma_2} + \gamma)(\bar{c} + \gamma) \bar{z}_\omega$$

The remaining terms of $r(X)$, along with a term for the $z$ opening, form the batched commitment:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[D]_1 = {} & \bar{a}\bar{b} [q_M]_1 + \bar{a} [q_L]_1 + \bar{b} [q_R]_1 + \bar{c} [q_O]_1 + [q_C]_1 \\
& + \big( (\bar{a} + \beta\zeta + \gamma)(\bar{b} + \beta k_1 \zeta + \gamma)(\bar{c} + \beta k_2 \zeta + \gamma)\alpha + L_1(\zeta)\alpha^2 + u \big) [z]_1 \\
& - (\bar{a} + \beta \bar{s}_{\sigma_1} + \gamma)(\bar{b} + \beta \bar{s}_{\sigma_2} + \gamma) \alpha\beta \bar{z}_\omega [s_{\sigma_3}]_1 \\
& - Z_H(\zeta) \big( [t_{lo}]_1 + \zeta^n [t_{mid}]_1 + \zeta^{2n} [t_{hi}]_1 \big)
\end{aligned}
$$

**5. Compute the full batched commitment $[F]_1$.** The verifier aggregates the commitments into a single point:

$$[F]_1 = [D]_1 + v [a]_1 + v^2 [b]_1 + v^3 [c]_1 + v^4 [s_{\sigma_1}]_1 + v^5 [s_{\sigma_2}]_1$$

**6. Compute the group-encoded batch evaluation $[E]_1$.** The verifier encodes the claimed evaluations into another group element:

$$[E]_1 = \left( -r_0 + v\bar{a} + v^2\bar{b} + v^3\bar{c} + v^4\bar{s}_{\sigma_1} + v^5\bar{s}_{\sigma_2} + u\bar{z}_\omega \right) [1]_1$$

**7. Pairing check.** The final step is a single pairing equation:

$$e\big([W_\zeta]_1 + u [W_{\zeta\omega}]_1,\ [x]_2\big) = e\big(\zeta [W_\zeta]_1 + u\zeta\omega [W_{\zeta\omega}]_1 + [F]_1 - [E]_1,\ [1]_2\big)$$

This confirms the consistency of all the evaluations, and through them, all the constraints.

### Why PLONK Verification Is Efficient

- **Batched evaluations:** Multiple checks are combined into one equation, needing just two pairings.
- **Scalability:** The work is independent of the circuit's size, apart from the public input evaluation, which grows with the number of public inputs.
- **Zero knowledge:** The verifier learns nothing beyond the validity of the proof.

By combining polynomial commitments, batched evaluations and pairings, PLONK provides a secure and scalable way to validate computations in zk-SNARKs.
